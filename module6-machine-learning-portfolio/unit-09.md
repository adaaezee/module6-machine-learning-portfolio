**Artefact:** CNNs and facial recognition: ethical and social implications (unit activity)

Unit 9 introduced convolutional neural networks, which learn visual features such as edges and shapes directly from images. The unit activity asked for a reflection on the ethical and social implications of CNN technology, based on Wall (2019).

Wall (2019) highlights significant ethical and social concerns surrounding the use of CNN-powered facial recognition technology, particularly in law enforcement. One issue that stood out to me was algorithmic bias. The article discusses how facial recognition systems can produce less accurate results for individuals with darker skin tones, particularly women. This raises concerns about fairness, as inaccurate identification could disproportionately affect certain demographic groups and potentially lead to wrongful accusations. 

Another concern is the invasion of privacy. The use of facial recognition in public spaces raises questions about consent, particularly when individuals are unaware that their biometric information is being collected and analysed. However, I recognise the potential benefits of this technology in improving public safety and identifying criminal suspects. In my view, the challenge is ensuring these benefits do not compromise fundamental human rights. 

Overall, I believe facial recognition technology should undergo rigorous testing across diverse demographic groups, with transparent governance, clear accountability and meaningful human oversight before being deployed in high-risk environments.
