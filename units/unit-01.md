**Artefact:** Industry 4.0 and the security sector: biometric access control (e-portfolio activity)

Technology has become a catalyst for global change, with Industry 4.0 representing the increasing integration of digital, physical and intelligent technologies across industries (Schwab, 2016). Technologies such as the Internet of Things (IoT), machine learning and robotics have transformed operational processes, improving automation, efficiency and decision-making. However, these advancements also introduce challenges that should not be overlooked. Within the security sector, Industry 4.0 has accelerated the transition from traditional, reactive monitoring towards interconnected, data-driven and increasingly predictive systems. For example, AI-powered video analytics can support faster threat detection, while biometric access control offers more convenient authentication methods.

Nevertheless, these benefits must be considered alongside their technical and ethical implications. Achimba et al. (2025) identify several limitations of biometric technologies, including sensitivity to environmental conditions, vulnerability to spoofing and difficulties generalising across diverse populations. Deep-learning approaches also introduce computational demands and limited interpretability, while privacy remains a concern across all biometric systems. These limitations have practical consequences, particularly where inaccurate identification could result in unfair access decisions or undermine user trust.

Of these concerns, privacy is particularly significant to me. Unlike passwords, compromised biometric characteristics cannot simply be replaced. I believe organisations should therefore prioritise transparency, interpretability and equitable performance across demographic groups rather than treating them as secondary considerations. This perspective also informed my use of SHAP and subgroup bias analysis in my [Unit 11 project](../projects/unit-11-individual-presentation/), reinforcing the importance of evaluating AI systems beyond predictive accuracy.
